\begin{abstract}
The Null-Vector Gravity (NVG) framework, anchored to a single QCD input
$M_{\Omega,0} = 859 \pm 8$~MeV fixed by lattice sigma terms, contains a
pseudo-Goldstone mode $\theta$ of the vacuum condensate $\Phi = W e^{i\theta}$.
We examine the phenomenological consequences of the hypothesis that the same
decay constant $f_a$ that sets the $\theta$-mode mass also generates Majorana
neutrino masses through a Weinberg-like dimension-five operator suppressed by
a two-loop QCD-anomaly coefficient. Introducing the unknown overall coefficient
of this operator as an explicit parameter $c_\nu$, the benchmark choice
$c_\nu = 1$ fixes $f_a = 1.07\times 10^{11}$~GeV from the neutrino sector and
yields a \emph{conditional point target} for dark matter: a coherent
pseudo-scalar of mass $m_\theta = 53.2~\mu\text{eV}$, detectable as a
microwave line at $f_\theta = 12.86$~GHz with photon coupling
$g_{\theta\gamma\gamma} = 2.08\times 10^{-14}$~GeV$^{-1}$ (KSVZ benchmark).
The product $m_\theta f_a$ agrees with the QCD axion band at 2.6\% --- a
normalization cross-check, not an independent test --- and the benchmark
frequency lies in the currently unexplored gap between the published ORGAN
mass windows ($25$--$26$ and $63$--$67~\mu$eV), directly on the lower edge of
the SMASH band ($50$--$200~\mu$eV) that ORGAN has declared as its roadmap
target. We present the derivation, a calibration of the haloscope signal
formula against the published ORGAN signal estimate, a Monte Carlo
propagation of the statistical input uncertainties ($m_3$,
$\chi_{\mathrm{top}}$), together with explicit conditional sensitivity
scenarios for the unknown coefficient $c_\nu$, and two concrete test
protocols: (A)~a
resonant-cavity array and (B)~a \emph{fixed-position} dielectric booster
stack, where the point target eliminates the scanning mechanism. For a
coherent dark-matter fraction of 24\% (misalignment only), protocol~B reaches
$\text{SNR}=5$ in ${\sim}\,18$~h at power boost $\beta^2 = 10^4$ (MADMAX
convention, $\beta^2 \equiv P_{\mathrm{sig}}/P_0$). The target is extended
to nucleon, electron and neutrino channels without introducing parameters
beyond the stated UV and flavor benchmark choices, and Appendix~\ref{app:uv}
gives a perturbative UV consistency
example for the $\theta$-seesaw (it does not derive the QCD-anomaly
suppression). Discovery and exclusion
criteria are preregistered. This is a \emph{phenomenological target paper}:
the $\theta$-seesaw operator is stated as a hypothesis, its coefficient is
propagated as a parameter, and every derived quantity is conditional on it.
All numerical values are reproducible from the accompanying scripts.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} axion dark matter, haloscope, dielectric haloscope,
pseudo-Goldstone boson, vacuum condensate, neutrino mass, phenomenological target
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Direct searches for QCD axion dark matter have so far produced exclusion limits
in disjoint mass windows: ADMX covers ${\sim}\,2.7$--$4.2~\mu$eV
\cite{ADMX2018}, HAYSTAC covers ${\sim}\,17$--$20~\mu$eV
\cite{HAYSTAC2025}, ORGAN has scanned $25.45$--$26.27~\mu$eV
\cite{ORGAN26}, $63.2$--$67.1~\mu$eV \cite{ORGAN2022} and
$107.42$--$111.93~\mu$eV \cite{ORGAN1b}, while MADMAX is under construction
for the $40$--$400~\mu$eV range \cite{MADMAX2019}. The intermediate region
around $50$--$60~\mu$eV ($12$--$15$~GHz) remains unexplored.

This note reports that the NVG/VMF framework \cite{NVGrepo}, anchored to a
single QCD input \cite{VMFanchor}, yields --- conditional on one stated
hypothesis (the $\theta$-seesaw, Sec.~\ref{sec:seesaw}) --- a point target
\emph{exactly} in that gap: a dark-matter pseudo-scalar at
$53.2~\mu$eV. Because the target is a \emph{point} in $(m, g_{\gamma})$
space rather than a band, the experimental problem simplifies drastically:
no scanning, no tuning mechanism --- a fixed-frequency integration.

Section~\ref{sec:theory} gives the derivation. Section~\ref{sec:signature}
states the observable signature. Section~\ref{sec:status} documents the gap
in published searches. Section~\ref{sec:protocol} defines the test protocol,
including a calibration of the signal formula against published data and
preregistered discovery/exclusion criteria.
Section~\ref{sec:interactions} extends the target to nucleon, electron and
neutrino channels; Appendix~\ref{app:uv} gives a perturbative UV
consistency example for the $\theta$-seesaw.
Section~\ref{sec:caveats}
lists the framework assumptions honestly.

\section{Theoretical Framework}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{The anchor: $M_{\Omega,0}$ from lattice sigma terms}

The framework is anchored to one dimensionful QCD input. Separating the nucleon
mass into current-quark and nonperturbative components via the Feynman--Hellmann
sigma terms ($\sigma_{\pi N} \approx 44$~MeV, $\sigma_{sN} \approx 30$~MeV),
the vacuum value of the nonperturbative component is fixed at
\cite{VMFanchor}
\begin{equation}
M_{\Omega,0} = 859 \pm 8~\text{MeV},
\end{equation}
which sets the condensate energy density scale
$\rho_c \approx 7.09\times 10^{4}$~MeV/fm$^{3}$ used throughout the
construction. No cosmological or astrophysical input is used at any stage.

\subsection{The $\theta$ pseudo-Goldstone mode}

The NVG vacuum condensate is parametrized as $\Phi = W\,e^{i\theta}$, where the
phase $\theta$ is a pseudo-Goldstone mode with the standard anomaly-generated
potential. Its mass obeys the same relation as the QCD axion,
\begin{equation}
m_\theta = \frac{\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm{top}}}}{f_a},
\label{eq:mtheta}
\end{equation}
with the topological susceptibility $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}^{1/4} = 75.5$~MeV from
lattice QCD \cite{QCDaxionPrecisely}. In the normalization
$m_a(f_a = 10^{12}~\text{GeV}) = 5.691~\mu$eV \cite{QCDaxionPrecisely}, this
becomes $m_\theta = 5.691~\mu\text{eV}\times (10^{12}~\text{GeV}/f_a)$.

\subsection{The $\theta$-seesaw: fixing $f_a$ from the neutrino sector}
\label{sec:seesaw}

The framework hypothesis behind the target is the \emph{$\theta$-seesaw}: the
same mode $\theta$ generates Majorana neutrino masses without right-handed
neutrinos. To be consistent with the electroweak gauge group above EWSB, the
operator must have the unique gauge-invariant dimension-five (Weinberg)
structure \cite{Weinberg1979}; the NVG hypothesis specifies only its
suppression scale and coefficient,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}} \;=\;
2\,c_\nu\left(\frac{\alpha_s}{4\pi}\right)^{\!2}\frac{1}{f_a}\,
\bigl(\tilde H^\dagger L\bigr)^{T} C\,
\bigl(\tilde H^\dagger L\bigr) + \text{h.c.},
\qquad \tilde H \equiv i\sigma_2 H^{*},
\label{eq:seesaw}
\end{equation}
where $c_\nu$ is an unknown dimensionless coefficient. The normalization
convention of this Weinberg-like operator, including symmetry factors in
flavor space and the factors of $1/\sqrt{2}$ from
$\langle H \rangle = v_{\mathrm{EW}}/\sqrt{2}$, is absorbed into $c_\nu$;
accordingly, $c_\nu = 1$ defines a benchmark normalization rather than a
UV-derived coefficient. The operator is \emph{defined} through the physical
post-EWSB relation, for the heaviest neutrino,
\begin{equation}
m_3 = c_\nu\left(\frac{\alpha_s}{4\pi}\right)^{\!2}\frac{v_{\mathrm{EW}}^{2}}{f_a}.
\label{eq:m3}
\end{equation}
With $\alpha_s(M_Z) = 0.1184$, $v_{\mathrm{EW}} = 246.22$~GeV and the
oscillation-and-cosmology anchor $m_3 = 50.3$~meV (normal ordering; the
implied sum $\sum m_\nu = 59$~meV passes the DESI DR2 bound
$\sum m_\nu < 64$~meV unconditionally), Eq.~\eqref{eq:m3} fixes
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;f_a = c_\nu\left(\frac{\alpha_s}{4\pi}\right)^{\!2}
\frac{v_{\mathrm{EW}}^{2}}{m_3} = 1.07\times 10^{11}\,c_\nu~\text{GeV}\;}
\label{eq:fa}
\end{equation}
We stress the status of Eq.~\eqref{eq:seesaw}: it is a \emph{framework
hypothesis}, the single non-standard ingredient of this note. The nominal
coefficient $(\alpha_s/4\pi)^2 \approx 8.9\times 10^{-5}$ is motivated by the
two-loop QCD-anomaly diagram, but possible flavor factors and RG running are
not computed here, and the UV completion that generates the operator is not
derived. The parameter $c_\nu$ absorbs all of this freedom: the benchmark
$c_\nu = 1$ defines the headline target, and every result below is rescaled
with $c_\nu$ (Section~\ref{sec:stats}). The haloscope test discriminates the
hypothesis directly (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}).

\subsection{Point target for the dark-matter mass}

Because every derived quantity depends on $c_\nu$ through $f_a$ alone, the
parametric structure is transparent (for fixed $E/N$):
\begin{equation}
f_a = f_{a,0}\,c_\nu,\qquad
m_\theta = \frac{m_{\theta,0}}{c_\nu},\qquad
f_\theta = \frac{f_{\theta,0}}{c_\nu},\qquad
g_{\theta\gamma\gamma} = \frac{g_{\theta\gamma\gamma,0}}{c_\nu},
\label{eq:scaling}
\end{equation}
where the subscript $0$ denotes the benchmark values at $c_\nu = 1$. A larger
$c_\nu$ means a larger $f_a$, hence a \emph{smaller} mass and frequency and a
weaker photon coupling. Inserting \eqref{eq:fa} into \eqref{eq:mtheta}:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;m_\theta = 5.691~\mu\text{eV}\times
\frac{10^{12}}{1.07\times 10^{11}\,c_\nu}
= \frac{53.2}{c_\nu}~\mu\text{eV}\;}
\end{equation}
or, equivalently, a Compton frequency
\begin{equation}
f_\theta = \frac{m_\theta c^2}{h} = \frac{12.86}{c_\nu}~\text{GHz}.
\label{eq:freq}
\end{equation}
The benchmark $c_\nu = 1$ gives $m_\theta = 53.2~\mu$eV, $f_\theta =
12.86$~GHz throughout the rest of this note.

\paragraph{Point versus band.} If a UV completion fixes $c_\nu$, the target
is a genuine \emph{conditional point} in $(m, g_\gamma, f)$ space. If $c_\nu$
remains free, the point degrades to a \emph{broad conditional search band}
along the one-parameter curve~\eqref{eq:scaling} --- $4.3$--$38.6$~GHz for
$c_\nu \in [1/3,\,3]$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:stats}) --- and the fixed-frequency
advantage is retained only conditionally, at a chosen benchmark value of
$c_\nu$. We use the terminology ``conditional point target'' and ``broad
conditional search band'' accordingly.

\paragraph{Normalization cross-check (not an independent test).} Once
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mtheta} is accepted, the product $m_\theta f_a =
\sqrt{\chi_{\mathrm{top}}}$ is fixed by the same QCD input, so its landing on
the QCD axion band $m_a f_a \simeq m_\pi f_\pi \sqrt{z}/(1+z) \approx
5.82\times 10^{15}$~eV$^2$ \cite{QCDaxionPrecisely} cannot count as an
independent confirmation of NVG or of the seesaw hypothesis. The framework
gives $m_\theta f_a = 5.67\times 10^{15}$~eV$^2$: the 2.6\% deviation is a
numerical consistency check of two lattice normalizations (the
$\chi_{\mathrm{top}}$ of \cite{QCDaxionPrecisely} against the VMF anchor),
and nothing more. The genuinely independent content of the target is the
\emph{value} of $f_a$ itself, fixed by the neutrino sector.

\subsection{Coupling to photons}

The effective interaction is the standard axion-electrodynamics term
\cite{Sikivie1983}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} \supset -\frac{g_{\theta\gamma\gamma}}{4}\,
\theta\, F_{\mu\nu}\tilde F^{\mu\nu}
= g_{\theta\gamma\gamma}\,\theta\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B},
\qquad
g_{\theta\gamma\gamma} = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\,
\frac{|E/N - 1.92|}{f_a}.
\label{eq:gagg}
\end{equation}
For the KSVZ benchmark \cite{KSVZ} ($E/N = 0$) and the DFSZ benchmark
\cite{DFSZ} ($E/N = 8/3$):
\begin{equation}
g_{\theta\gamma\gamma}^{\mathrm{KSVZ}} = 2.08\times 10^{-14}~\text{GeV}^{-1},
\qquad
g_{\theta\gamma\gamma}^{\mathrm{DFSZ}} = 8.10\times 10^{-15}~\text{GeV}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The model-dependent factor $|E/N - 1.92|$ is the only residual theory
uncertainty on the coupling. We emphasize that $g_{\theta\gamma\gamma}$ is
\emph{not predicted} by NVG: the framework fixes $f_a$ but not the UV anomaly
ratio $E/N$, so KSVZ and DFSZ are benchmarks, not consequences of the model.
A null result at KSVZ level therefore excludes only KSVZ-like realizations
(Section~\ref{sec:stats}). The KSVZ value is used as the discovery benchmark.

\subsection{Relic abundance: the honest coherent fraction}
\label{sec:abundance}

For a post-inflationary scenario with the natural initial misalignment
$\theta_i = \pi/\sqrt{3}$, the standard misalignment estimate
\cite{Misalignment} gives
\begin{equation}
\Omega_\theta h^2 \simeq 0.12\,\theta_i^2
\left(\frac{f_a}{10^{12}~\text{GeV}}\right)^{7/6}
= 0.0291,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ \textbf{24\% of the local dark-matter density}
($\Omega_{\mathrm{DM}} h^2 = 0.120$). The remaining density may reside in
topological defects of the condensate sector; it does not contribute to the
coherent haloscope signal. All signal powers below use the conservative coherent
fraction $f_{\mathrm{coh}} = 0.24$; the $f_{\mathrm{coh}}=1$ values are also
quoted where relevant. A larger coherent fraction (e.g.\ from defect decay)
would strengthen the signal proportionally.

\subsection{Fifth force: the pseudoscalar null channel}

The Compton range $\lambda_\theta = \hbar c/m_\theta = 3.72$~mm falls inside
the sub-millimetre window of torsion-balance experiments \cite{AxionReview}.
Because $\theta$ is a \emph{pseudo}scalar, the leading-order
monopole--monopole Yukawa exchange between unmixed states vanishes, so
unpolarized E\"ot-Wash-class searches are expected to see nothing at this
range. The residual channels --- spin-dipole exchange with coupling
$m_N/f_a$, loop-induced scalar admixtures, two-boson exchange, or
condensate mixing --- require a dedicated analysis and are not claimed here
as quantitative predictions; we state only the robust null expectation for
the unpolarized monopole channel.

\section{Multi-Channel Interaction Block}
\label{sec:interactions}

The photon coupling of Sec.~\ref{sec:theory} is one entry of the full
low-energy interaction block. Completing it makes the target multi-channel
falsifiable without introducing parameters beyond the stated UV and flavor
benchmark choices: the fermion axial
charges below are fixed either by the same canonical UV benchmark choice
--- $E/N = 0$ with $C_q^{0} = 0$ (KSVZ-like), or the DFSZ-like benchmark
with a stated $\tan\beta$ --- already made for the photon channel, or ---
in the neutrino channel --- by the
$\theta$-seesaw operator itself.

\subsection{Effective Lagrangian and channel benchmarks}

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}} =
-\frac{g_{\theta\gamma\gamma}}{4}\,\theta\,F\tilde F
+ \frac{\partial_\mu\theta}{2f_a}\sum_{f} C_f\,
\bar f \gamma^\mu\gamma^5 f,
\label{eq:lint}
\end{equation}
with $f = e, p, n, \nu_3$. Using the fermion equations of motion, each
derivative coupling is equivalent to a pseudoscalar Yukawa coupling
$g_{\theta ff} = C_f\, m_f/f_a$.

\paragraph{Nucleons.} The axial charges split into a model-independent
chiral piece (axion--pion mixing, fixed by QCD) and the UV charges
$C_q^{0}$ \cite{AxionLandscape}:
\begin{equation}
C_p = -0.47 + 0.88\,C_u^{0} - 0.39\,C_d^{0} - 0.49\,C_s^{0},\qquad
C_n = -0.02 - 0.02\,C_u^{0} + 0.88\,C_d^{0} - 0.49\,C_s^{0}.
\label{eq:ccharges}
\end{equation}
For the KSVZ-like benchmark ($C_q^{0} = 0$ --- the same UV choice as
$E/N = 0$), $C_p = -0.47$ and $C_n = -0.02$ are \emph{predictions}, not
parameters: $g_{\theta pp} = -4.1\times 10^{-12}$ and
$g_{\theta nn} = -1.8\times 10^{-13}$. The spin-dependent fifth-force
channel of Sec.~\ref{sec:signature} is thereby upgraded from the order-of-magnitude
quote $m_N/f_a$ to a definite coupling.

\paragraph{Electrons.} The two photon benchmarks separate here: DFSZ-like
UV gives a tree-level $C_e = \cos^{2}\!\beta/3$ (illustratively
$0.033$ at $\tan\beta = 3$, $g_{\theta ee} \simeq 1.6\times 10^{-16}$;
smaller $\tan\beta$ raises $C_e$ up to $1/3$), while KSVZ-like UV gives
$C_e = 0$ at tree level and a loop-suppressed
$C_e \sim 10^{-5}$ ($g_{\theta ee} \sim 5\times 10^{-20}$). The resulting
${\sim}\,3$--$4$-order spread makes the electron channel a UV
discriminator. Both values pass
the white-dwarf cooling constraint $g_{ae} \lesssim 3\times 10^{-13}$
\cite{AxionReview} with wide margins.

\paragraph{Neutrinos.} The $\theta$-seesaw operator itself carries the
PQ phase, so the heaviest-state mass term reads $m_3\,e^{i\theta/f_a}$:
the heaviest neutrino obtains an axial charge $C_{\nu_3} = 1$ in the
normalization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:seesaw}, i.e.\
$g_{\theta\nu_3\nu_3} \simeq m_3/f_a =
4.7\times 10^{-22}$. This coupling is a distinctive NVG signature --- it is
absent in plain KSVZ and DFSZ --- and the decay $\theta\to\nu\nu$ is
kinematically forbidden ($2m_3 \gg m_\theta$), so the channel affects
stability of the dark-matter candidate not at all. The solar mass $m_2$,
generated by a second, PQ-neutral effective operator whose UV completion is
not specified (Sec.~\ref{sec:flavor}), is independent of the
$m_3$--$f_a$ benchmark relation and carries no such coupling.

\subsection{Minimal normal-ordering flavor benchmark}
\label{sec:flavor}

The two measured mass splittings require at least two massive light
neutrinos. Accordingly, the atmospheric mass $m_3$ is generated by a
PQ-carrying heavy state $N_3$
(and hence fixes $f_a$ and the haloscope benchmark via
Eq.~\eqref{eq:m3}), while the solar mass $m_2$ is attributed to a second,
PQ-neutral effective Weinberg operator whose UV completion is not
specified, since it does not enter the $m_3$--$f_a$ benchmark relation.
With
$m_1 \simeq 0$, this \emph{minimal normal-ordering benchmark} and the
NuFIT~5.2 oscillation parameters \cite{NuFIT}
yield the correlated triple
\begin{equation}
\sum m_\nu = 58.9~\text{meV},\qquad
m_\beta = 8.8~\text{meV},\qquad
m_{\beta\beta} \in [1.5,\,3.7]~\text{meV},
\label{eq:rank1}
\end{equation}
where the $0\nu\beta\beta$ value carries a \emph{nonzero floor}
($|U_{e2}^{2} m_2 - U_{e3}^{2} m_3|$ at destructive Majorana phase).
The inverted-ordering alternative gives
$\sum m_\nu \approx 106$~meV, so the minimal-NO hypothesis is a clean
cosmological discriminator: it places the mass sum at the normal-ordering
minimum, and a measurement $\sum m_\nu \gtrsim 75$~meV (CMB-S4/DESI-class
sensitivity) would exclude it. As with the $c_\nu$ scenarios of
Sec.~\ref{sec:stats}, the minimal spectrum and the solar-sector operator are
scenario assumptions, not framework derivations.

\subsection{Experimental mapping}

The predicted nucleon couplings fall inside the design window of
NMR/spin-precession programs: the CASPEr design band
$f_a \sim 10^{9}$--$10^{12}$~GeV \cite{CASPEr} contains
$f_a = 1.07\times 10^{11}$~GeV, and at $m_\theta = 53.2~\mu$eV the
oscillating nuclear EDM channel (CASPEr-electric type) and the
spin--gradient channel (CASPEr-wind, QUAX, ARIADNE type) address the same
mass with a \emph{predicted} coupling rather than a scanned one. The
electron channel is a UV discriminator rather than a prediction; the
fifth-force channel of Sec.~\ref{sec:signature} remains qualitative pending
a computation of the loop-induced scalar admixture.

\section{Test Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Signal-power formula and its calibration}
\label{sec:calib}

For a microwave cavity haloscope \cite{Sikivie1983,Egge2023},
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{sig}} = g_{\theta\gamma\gamma}^{2}\,
\frac{\rho_\theta}{m_\theta}\,B_0^{2}\,V\,C\,\min(Q_L, Q_a),
\label{eq:psig}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_\theta = f_{\mathrm{coh}}\,\rho_{\mathrm{DM}}$ with
$\rho_{\mathrm{DM}} = 0.4~\text{GeV/cm}^3$, $V$ is the cavity volume,
$C$ the mode form factor, and $Q_L$ the loaded quality factor.

\textbf{Calibration against published data.} The ORGAN collaboration quotes,
for its Phase~1a apparatus, $P_{\mathrm{sig}} \simeq 2.3\times 10^{-25}$~W
(KSVZ) and $3.1\times 10^{-26}$~W (DFSZ) for a $64~\mu$eV axion
\cite{ORGAN2022}. Two checks:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Apparatus-independent}: the coupling-coefficient ratio
$(g_{\mathrm{KSVZ}}/g_{\mathrm{DFSZ}})^2 = (0.97/0.36)^2 = 7.26$ versus the
published power ratio $2.3/0.31 = 7.42$ --- agreement at the 2\% level,
confirming the normalization convention of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gagg}.
\item \emph{Absolute}: back-solving Eq.~\eqref{eq:psig} from the published
$2.3\times 10^{-25}$~W implies an apparatus product $V C Q \approx 0.44$~m$^3$
(for $B_0 = 7$~T), consistent with an ORGAN-scale cavity at 15.6~GHz with
$Q \sim 10^{4}$--$10^{5}$. The unit chain of the present calculation is thus
validated against a real haloscope at the factor-of-two level.
\end{enumerate}

For the dielectric booster concept \cite{MADMAX2017,MADMAXtheory,MADMAX2019} the
analogous expression is
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{sig}} = g_{\theta\gamma\gamma}^{2}\,\rho_\theta\,B_0^{2}\,
\frac{A\,\beta^{2}}{m_\theta^{2}},
\label{eq:pstack}
\end{equation}
where $A$ is the disk area and $\beta^{2} \equiv P_{\mathrm{sig}}/P_0$ is the
\emph{power} boost relative to a bare magnetized mirror
($P_0 = g_{\theta\gamma\gamma}^{2}\rho_\theta B_0^{2} A/m_\theta^{2}$),
following the MADMAX convention \cite{MADMAXproto} in which $\beta$ itself
denotes the amplitude boost; all boost figures quoted in this note are power
boosts $\beta^2$. Both Eq.~\eqref{eq:psig} and \eqref{eq:pstack} have been
re-derived within the Lorentz-reciprocity framework \cite{Egge2023,Egge2024exp};
an in-situ reciprocity measurement with a source antenna is the final
normalization check of the stack before any dark-matter run.

\subsection{Protocol B: fixed-position dielectric booster stack}
\label{sec:stack}

The dielectric haloscope \cite{MADMAX2017} replaces the resonant cavity by
${\sim}80$ dielectric disks (LaAlO$_3$, $\varepsilon \approx 24$) in a strong
magnetic field; constructive interference of the emitted photons provides the
power boost $\beta^2$. In a scanning experiment the disk positions must be
moved mechanically --- the technically hardest subsystem, now being
commissioned \cite{MADMAXmech}. \textbf{The point-frequency target eliminates
this subsystem entirely}: at a known frequency the inter-disk spacing is
fixed, and the stack becomes a static optical element.

With $D = 1.2$~m disks ($A = 1.13$~m$^2$, the low-mass-end MADMAX aperture),

(Table~\ref{tab:stack}). The power-boost targets $\beta^2 = 10^4$--$10^5$ are
the design goals of the MADMAX program \cite{MADMAX2019}; the three-disk

\subsection{Readout, statistics, and preregistered criteria}
\label{sec:stats}

\paragraph{Noise.} $T_{\mathrm{sys}} \approx 1$~K is adopted: millikelvin
physical temperature plus a flux-driven Josephson parametric amplifier. This is
not an assumption but demonstrated technology --- ORGAN's most recent phase
operates exactly this way \cite{ORGAN26}.

\paragraph{Statistical target definition.} With the Dicke radiometer relation
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SNR} = \frac{P_{\mathrm{sig}}}{k_B T_{\mathrm{sys}}}
\sqrt{\frac{t}{\Delta f}},
\end{equation}
the narrow search window below is conditional on the benchmark coefficient
$c_\nu = 1$. Statistical uncertainties in $m_3$ and $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}$
are propagated by Monte Carlo (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}: $m_3 \sim
\mathcal{N}(50.3,\,0.25)$~meV, $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}^{1/4} \sim
\mathcal{N}(75.5,\,0.7\%)$~MeV), while $c_\nu$ is \emph{not} assigned a
statistical prior: it is an explicit theory parameter. Broader intervals
obtained by varying $c_\nu$ below are sensitivity scenarios, not confidence
intervals.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Conditional benchmark window ($c_\nu = 1$ fixed).} The
target-frequency distribution has median $12.86$~GHz and a 68\% interval
$[12.67,\,13.05]$~GHz (relative width $1.5\%$, dominated by
$\chi_{\mathrm{top}}$). The corresponding pre-specified $3\sigma$ analysis
window is $[12.29,\,13.43]$~GHz, resolved in bins of width $\Delta f_a$.
\item \textbf{Illustrative coefficient scenario ($c_\nu$ varied over
$[1/2,\,2]$).} Via Eq.~\eqref{eq:scaling} this non-statistical sensitivity
scenario broadens the target range to $6.4$--$25.7$~GHz. It is a scan, not a
point-target test, and must carry its full trials factor and sensitivity
variation across the band.
\item \textbf{Extreme envelope ($c_\nu \in [1/3,\,3]$).} $f_\theta \in
[4.3,\,38.6]$~GHz ($\sim$9 times the benchmark band): the full scan cost if
$c_\nu$ is never fixed by theory.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{Signal power and sensitivity across the band.} Along the
curve~\eqref{eq:scaling}, \emph{at fixed coherent fraction}, the signal
powers scale simply: $P_{\mathrm{sig}}^{\mathrm{stack}} \propto g^2/m_\theta^2
\propto c_\nu^{0}$ for the dielectric stack (exactly invariant under $c_\nu$
rescaling) and $P_{\mathrm{sig}}^{\mathrm{cavity}} \propto g^2/m_\theta
\propto c_\nu^{-1}$ for the cavity, so the integration times of
Tables~\ref{tab:arrays}--\ref{tab:stack} apply unchanged to the stack and
rescale as $c_\nu^2$ for the cavity at any other benchmark value of $c_\nu$.
If instead the misalignment estimate of Sec.~\ref{sec:abundance} is imposed,
the coherent fraction itself scales as $f_{\mathrm{coh}} \propto f_a^{7/6}
\propto c_\nu^{7/6}$, which multiplies both powers accordingly. Across
coefficient scenarios the required sensitivity is therefore not constant: at
fixed $E/N$, $g_{\theta\gamma\gamma} \propto c_\nu^{-1}$, while the assumed
misalignment abundance also changes with $f_a \propto c_\nu$; consequently, a
broad-$c_\nu$ program requires a frequency-dependent sensitivity forecast
rather than a uniform scan-time rescaling. The $\pm 0.5\%$ window of the v1
draft covered only $m_3$ and $\chi_{\mathrm{top}}$ and is superseded.

\paragraph{Trials factor and global significance.} The benchmark prediction
is a \emph{conditional narrow target} in model-parameter space, but the
experimentally searched frequency interval still contains many statistically
independent axion-line templates: the $1.14$~GHz benchmark window holds
$N_{\mathrm{eff}} \simeq 8.9\times 10^{4}$ independent bins of width
$\Delta f_a = 12.8$~kHz. A local $5\sigma$ excess scanned over the whole
window corresponds to only ${\sim}\,2\sigma$ globally, so the discovery
statistic must report both a local significance and a global significance
corrected for the pre-specified $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Pre-registration fixes
the analysis window and procedure; it does not remove the trials correction.

\paragraph{Discovery criterion.} A candidate must exceed a pre-specified
local threshold corresponding to a \emph{global} $5\sigma$ false-alarm
probability after correction for $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ templates in the
benchmark window. For the conservative independent-bin estimate above,
$N_{\mathrm{eff}} \simeq 8.9\times 10^{4}$ implies a local threshold of
approximately $6.9\sigma$. The final $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ shall be obtained
from the matched-filter bank and verified with noise-only Monte Carlo. The
candidate must also persist under independent re-taking, scale as $B_0^2$
when the magnet current is varied, match the expected Maxwell--Boltzmann halo
lineshape, and be reproduced at a second apparatus with a different magnet
before any claim.

\paragraph{Exclusion criterion.} Exclusion is reported at two levels.
(1)~A null result at KSVZ-level sensitivity,
$g_{\theta\gamma\gamma} \le 2.1\times 10^{-14}$~GeV$^{-1}$ across the
preregistered window, excludes the \emph{KSVZ-benchmark realization} of the
$\theta$-seesaw target at $f_{\mathrm{coh}} = 0.24$ --- and only that
realization, since $E/N$ is UV-dependent. (2)~A null at DFSZ level
($8.1\times 10^{-15}$~GeV$^{-1}$) additionally covers the DFSZ benchmark;
excluding the neutrino--axion linkage itself would further require a fixed
$E/N$ assignment and a cosmological determination of $f_{\mathrm{coh}}$.
Because exclusion curves assume the full local density, the effective bound on
$g$ for a coherent fraction $f_{\mathrm{coh}}$ is weaker by
$1/\sqrt{f_{\mathrm{coh}}} \approx 2.0$; this rescaling must be applied when
interpreting any null.

\paragraph{Side-channel cross-checks.} The same $f_a$ predicts
$\sum m_\nu = 59$~meV (CMB+BAO, ongoing) and, in the general case, a
$0\nu\beta\beta$ window $m_{\beta\beta} \in [0,\, 6.4]$~meV, narrowing to
the nonzero range $[1.5,\,3.7]$~meV under the minimal normal-ordering
benchmark of
Sec.~\ref{sec:flavor} (LEGEND-1000, nEXO): these provide
framework-level corroboration independent of haloscopes.

\section{Honest Caveats}
\label{sec:caveats}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Framework hypothesis and its coefficient.} The $\theta$-seesaw,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:seesaw}, is a postulate of the NVG construction. The
coefficient $c_\nu$ --- encoding the two-loop
anomaly diagram, possible flavor factors and RG running --- is unknown and is
propagated as a parameter throughout; $c_\nu = 1$ is a benchmark, not a
derived value. Appendix~\ref{app:uv} gives a perturbative UV consistency
example --- a minimal seesaw whose PQ-carrying state $N_3$ generates
$m_3$, with $m_2$ attributed to an unspecified PQ-neutral sector --- and
shows that the
benchmark corresponds to a perturbative, $\tau$-Yukawa-scale neutrino
coupling with ${\sim}\,5$--$10\%$ RG running. It does \emph{not} derive the
two-loop QCD-anomaly suppression of Eq.~\eqref{eq:seesaw}, which remains
part of the effective hypothesis, and it does not fix $c_\nu$.
If Eq.~\eqref{eq:m3} fails --- e.g.\
$m_3$ from a future
0$\nu\beta\beta$/oscillation combination disagrees with $50.3$~meV --- the
entire frequency target moves. The haloscope test is precisely the
discriminator.
\item \textbf{Spectral premise.} The input $m_3 = 50.3$~meV assumes normal
ordering and a near-minimal lightest neutrino mass. Oscillation experiments
measure only mass-squared differences, and cosmology bounds the sum of masses
without fixing $m_3$ at this precision. The target is therefore conditional on
this premise; the Monte Carlo window of Sec.~\ref{sec:stats} covers the
stated uncertainty, and a shift to inverted ordering or a heavier lightest
state would move the target outside the benchmark window.
\item \textbf{Model dependence of the coupling.} The factor
$|E/N - 1.92|$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:gagg} is benchmarked to KSVZ/DFSZ; a
different UV completion rescales $g_{\theta\gamma\gamma}$ without moving the
frequency. The mass target is unaffected; the coupling is not predicted.
\item \textbf{Coherent fraction.} All powers use $f_{\mathrm{coh}} = 0.24$
(misalignment only). If most of the sector's density sits in incoherent defects,
haloscope signals weaken accordingly; if defects decay into coherent modes, they
strengthen. This is the dominant astrophysical uncertainty and is why the
exclusion criterion carries the $1/\sqrt{f_{\mathrm{coh}}}$ rescaling.
\item \textbf{Technology risk.} $Q_L = 10^6$ at 13~GHz and $\beta^2 = 10^5$
are program goals, not demonstrated hardware (the MADMAX prototype reached
$\beta^2 \sim 2000$). The baseline rows ($Q_L = 10^5$, $\beta^2 = 10^4$)
reflect the validated state of the art.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}

This note is a phenomenological target paper. Under the stated
$\theta$-seesaw hypothesis with benchmark coefficient $c_\nu = 1$, a single
neutrino-sector input of the NVG/VMF framework fixes
$f_a = 1.07\times 10^{11}$~GeV, which through lattice-QCD physics yields a
conditional point target: $m_\theta = 53.2~\mu$eV, a microwave line at
$12.86$~GHz with KSVZ-level photon coupling. The point lands on the QCD axion
band within 2.6\% (a normalization cross-check, not an independent test),
sits in the currently unexplored gap between published ORGAN windows, and is
directly on that collaboration's declared SMASH roadmap. Because the target is
a point, the search needs no scanning: a fixed dielectric booster stack
reaches discovery-level significance in under a day of integration at design
power boost $\beta^2 = 10^4$. The protocol above is preregistered, the
frequency window is derived by Monte Carlo from all stated uncertainties, and
the exclusion statements are scoped precisely: a KSVZ-level null closes the
KSVZ-benchmark realization of the target, not every conceivable
$\theta$-model. Either outcome is informative --- which is the property a
target must have to deserve an experiment.

\section*{Appendix B: A Perturbative UV Consistency Example for the $\theta$-Seesaw}
\label{app:uv}

This appendix shows that the benchmark coefficient $c_\nu = 1$ is
\emph{perturbatively viable} --- realizable by non-hierarchical UV physics ---
and quantifies the RG running absorbed into $c_\nu$. Its status is that of a
consistency example: it does \emph{not} derive the two-loop QCD-anomaly
suppression of Eq.~\eqref{eq:seesaw}, which remains part of the effective
NVG hypothesis encoded in $c_\nu$, and it does not fix $c_\nu$.

\paragraph{Field content and PQ charges.} A global $U(1)_{\mathrm{PQ}}$ is
spontaneously broken by a scalar $\sigma$ (charge $X_\sigma = 2$) at the
scale $f_a$; the phase of $\sigma$ is $\theta$. The UV completion specified
here concerns the atmospheric mass $m_3$ alone, as required by the
$m_3$--$f_a$ benchmark relation (the solar mass $m_2$ is attributed to a
separate sector, below):
\begin{itemize}
\item $N_3$ ($X_{N_3} = 1$), with Majorana mass from
$\frac{1}{2}y_\sigma\sigma^{\dagger} N_3^{T}CN_3$
(PQ-invariant: $-2 + 1 + 1 = 0$):
$M_3 = y_\sigma f_a/\sqrt{2} \equiv \kappa f_a$, carrying the PQ phase
$M_3(\theta) = M_3\,e^{-i\theta/f_a}$. Integrating $N_3$ out generates the
dominant atmospheric mass $m_3$ and hence fixes $f_a$;
\item a second, PQ-neutral effective Weinberg operator is assumed to
generate the solar mass $m_2$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:flavor}). Its UV completion is
not specified, since it does not enter the $m_3$--$f_a$ benchmark relation;
\item lepton doublets and charged-lepton singlets with $X_L = X_{e_R} = 1$,
so that both $\bar L \tilde H N_3$ and $\bar L H e_R$ are PQ-invariant while
quarks and $H$ are neutral;
\item one vectorlike color-triplet $Q$ (KSVZ-like, electromagnetically
neutral), which makes $U(1)_{\mathrm{PQ}}$ anomalous under color and yields
$E/N = 0$ --- the photon benchmark of Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Generation of the Weinberg operator.} Integrating out $N_3$ at
tree level through the standard seesaw diagram
$L\tilde H$--$N_3$--$\tilde H^{\dagger}L^{\dagger}$ produces
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{W} = \frac{y_3^{2}}{2M_3}\,
\frac{\sigma}{|\sigma|}\,
\bigl(\tilde H^{\dagger}L\bigr)^{T}C\bigl(\tilde H^{\dagger}L\bigr)
+ \text{h.c.},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the Weinberg operator of Eq.~\eqref{eq:seesaw} with its PQ phase
$e^{i\theta/f_a}$ generated automatically (convention-consistent with the
sign choice of Eq.~\eqref{eq:m3}). Matching to the normalization
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:m3} gives
\begin{equation}
c_\nu = \frac{y_3^{2}}{2\kappa\,(\alpha_s/4\pi)^{2}}
= 5.6\times 10^{3}\,\frac{y_3^{2}}{\kappa}.
\label{eq:matching}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{What the matching does --- and does not --- imply.}
The benchmark $c_\nu = 1$ with $\kappa = 1$ corresponds to
$y_3 = 1.3\times 10^{-2} \approx 1.8\,y_\tau$: a perturbative Yukawa of
$\tau$-lepton magnitude, with no hierarchy and no fine-tuning. The scenario
range $c_\nu \in [1/3,\,3]$ corresponds to
$y_3 \in [7.7\times 10^{-3},\,2.3\times 10^{-2}]/\sqrt{\kappa}$, i.e.\ a
factor of ${\sim}\,2$ around $y_\tau$. Perturbativity
($y_3^2 \lesssim 4\pi$) alone bounds
$c_\nu \lesssim 7\times 10^{4}/\kappa$, which is not restrictive: absent
additional flavor dynamics, $c_\nu$ is not calculable, and the haloscope
test of Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol} remains the discriminator.
Crucially, the minimal seesaw completion demonstrates only that the
benchmark normalization is perturbative; it does \emph{not} derive the
proposed QCD-anomaly suppression $(\alpha_s/4\pi)^{2}$ --- in a plain
tree-level seesaw that factor does not arise, and it remains part of the
effective NVG hypothesis encoded in $c_\nu$. The colored multiplet $Q$
generates the PQ--QCD anomaly of the $\theta$ mode but is not connected to
the $L$--$H$--$N_3$ diagram above.

\paragraph{RG running.} The one-loop running of the Weinberg coefficient in
the Standard Model \cite{AntuschRG},
\begin{equation}
16\pi^{2}\frac{dC_W}{d\ln\mu} =
\bigl(-3g_2^{2} + 6y_t^{2} + \lambda\bigr)C_W
+ \bigl(Y_e^{\dagger}Y_e\bigr)C_W + C_W\bigl(Y_e^{\dagger}Y_e\bigr)^{T},
\end{equation}
shifts the coefficient by ${\sim}\,5$--$10\%$ between $M_3 = f_a$ and the
electroweak scale; this is absorbed into $c_\nu$ and is subleading relative
to the benchmark scenario granularity.

\paragraph{Neutrino coupling and domain walls.} The phase
$e^{i\theta/f_a}$ in the $m_3$ term yields the axial coupling
$C_{\nu_3} = 1$ ($g_{\theta\nu_3\nu_3} \simeq m_3/f_a$) used in
Sec.~\ref{sec:interactions} --- the NVG-specific signature absent in plain
KSVZ/DFSZ, carried by the $N_3$ sector alone. The colored sector carries
unit anomaly, so the domain-wall
number is $N_{\mathrm{DW}} = 1$ and any walls formed in the post-inflationary
scenario are unstable, consistent with the cosmology of
Sec.~\ref{sec:abundance}.
